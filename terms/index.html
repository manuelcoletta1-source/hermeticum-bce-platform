Terms of use for HBCE Platform, IPR Card and JOKER-C2.

HBCE Platform is an experimental public gateway for research, documentation, technical verification, audit trails, IPR onboarding, proof references and fail-closed governance.

The current architecture is centered on HBCE Platform as the IPR Onboarding Gateway, IPR as the first operational product, IPR Card as the verified operational access key, JOKER-C2 as the AI Operational Runtime, HBCE as the governance ecosystem and MATRIX as the wider architectural framework.

The central access rule is: JOKER-C2 access requires a valid operational IPR state where policy allows it.

PLATFORM STATUS

R&D, MVP and pilot boundary

HBCE Platform is currently an experimental public research and development infrastructure. Some pages describe conceptual, prototype, demonstrative, pilot-positioning or planned components. Unless expressly stated otherwise, these components must not be interpreted as production controls, formal certifications, regulated services, qualified trust services or final operational guarantees.

The platform documents and tests models for operational identity, IPR-linked evidence, minimized public registry records, technical verification, private identity verification, IPR Card issuance, EVT continuity, OPC proof and fail-closed governance.

IPR is not a public legal identity and does not replace SPID, CIE, EUDI Wallet, eIDAS schemes, public registries, digital signatures or qualified trust services. IPR is treated as an operational record for verifiable events, proof material, access state and controlled workflows.

PUBLIC SERVICES AND SURFACES

Platform surfaces

  • Onboarding — operational entry path for registration, verification preparation and IPR state transition.
  • Create — preparation surface for IPR_DRAFT and IPR_PENDING records.
  • Activate — review and activation surface for moving toward IPR_ACTIVE where allowed.
  • IPR Card — operational card model for active, pending, suspended, revoked or expired IPR states.

  • Verify — public verification using fail-closed logic, registry checks and SHA-256 comparison.
