Terms of Use

HBCE Platform is an experimental public gateway for research, documentation, technical verification, audit trails and fail-closed governance. The current scope is centered on IPR as the operational R&D/MVP product, IPR AI Audit Trail as the first demonstrable use case, HBCE as the operational ecosystem, JOKER-C2 as the runtime demonstrator and MATRiX as the architectural framework.

Use of the platform must remain lawful, defensive, authorized, auditable and consistent with the separation between minimized public proof and private evidence.

Policy: EU-first · audit-first · hash-oriented evidence · append-only discipline · fail-closed · GDPR-min · no public data custody

1. Platform status

HBCE Platform is currently an experimental public research and development infrastructure. Some pages describe conceptual, prototype, demonstrative or planned components. Unless expressly stated otherwise, these components must not be interpreted as production controls, formal certifications, regulated services, qualified trust services or final operational guarantees.

The platform documents and tests models for operational identity, IPR-linked evidence, minimized public registry records, technical verification, EVT continuity, OPC proof and fail-closed governance.

IPR is not a public legal identity, does not replace SPID, CIE, EUDI Wallet, eIDAS schemes, public registries, digital signatures or qualified trust services. In the current phase, IPR is treated as an R&D/MVP operational record for verifiable events, proof material and workflows.

2. System hierarchy

IPR

IPR — Identity Primary Record — is the base operational product. It is designed to connect an operational identity reference, an event context, a responsibility layer, proof material and a verification path.

IPR AI Audit Trail

IPR AI Audit Trail is the first MVP use case. It demonstrates how an AI-assisted document workflow can be connected to IPR, document hashing, governance decisioning, human validation, EVT, OPC and an audit-ready report.

HBCE

HBCE is the operational ecosystem containing policy logic, verification surfaces, registry references, evidence packs, event continuity, proof records and fail-closed rules.

JOKER-C2 and MATRiX

JOKER-C2 is the runtime demonstrator used to show IPR in operation inside HBCE. MATRiX is the broader architectural framework. Neither should be presented as a public authority, regulated service or standalone legal certification system.

3. Public services and surfaces

  • IPR — product page for the operational record connecting identity, event, proof and continuity.
  • Create — surfaces for creating or preparing IPR records, payloads, proof commitments and receipts.
  • Registry — minimized public registry based on technical records and payload_sha256.
  • Verify — public verification using fail-closed logic, registry v3 checks and SHA-256 comparison.
  • IPR AI Audit Trail — MVP for AI-assisted document workflows with human validation and audit trails.
  • Schemas — public schemas for document validation and proof commitments.

A match in the public registry indicates the presence of a public proof commitment. It does not replace private evidence, legal validation, institutional authorization, private identity verification, contractual qualification, regulated certification or approval by a public authority.

4. Hash-oriented and no-custody rule

The public layer of the platform must use minimized records and hash-oriented references. The public registry must not custody private evidence, identity documents, raw personal identifiers, customer data, credentials, secrets, private keys, private prompts, private outputs, production logs or sensitive operational payloads.

If verification requires private or sensitive material, that verification must take place outside the public layer through authorized, private channels that comply with applicable law.

5. Fail-closed

Fail-closed logic means that the platform must not produce implicit validity when a required condition is absent, ambiguous, unverifiable, inconsistent or malformed.

  • missing input → INVALID;
  • malformed hash → INVALID;
  • unreadable registry → NON_OPERATIONAL;
  • missing record → NO_PUBLIC_RECORD;
  • non-compliant schema → INVALID or NON_OPERATIONAL;
  • sensitive data in the public layer → block, removal or review.

6. Permitted use

Permitted use of the platform includes research, documentation, hash-oriented verification, defensive audit activity, modeling of minimized public registries, responsible AI governance, operational identity research and testing with owned, authorized, public or synthetic materials.

Users must use the platform lawfully, defensively, with authorization, proportionally and consistently with the privacy, security, governance and R&D/MVP limits documented in the repository.

8. Public demos and allowed materials

Public demos and R&D tests must use only synthetic, public, owned or expressly authorized materials. Users must not upload or paste identity documents, customer personal data, secrets, credentials, private keys, confidential business data, unauthorized third-party data, production logs or regulated evidence requiring protected handling.

Any external pilot involving real data requires a separate environment with legal basis, responsibility allocation, security controls, privacy controls, retention rules, access controls and contractual scope defined before use.

9. Operational receipts

Any operational receipts, proof receipts, EVT records, OPC proofs or JSON files generated by the platform must be interpreted as technical or documentary artifacts connected to verification, traceability and workflow reconstruction.

These receipts do not automatically constitute invoices, legal certification, regulated qualification, institutional authorization, private identity proof, automated compliance or standalone legal evidentiary value. Any fiscal, contractual, legal or regulatory obligations must be handled separately under applicable law.

10. User responsibility

The user is responsible for the materials they enter, upload, store, publish or use in connection with the platform. Users must not place unnecessary personal data, secrets, private keys, credentials, identity documents, private evidence, customer data, unauthorized third-party data or sensitive payloads in the public layer.

The user must verify that they have the right or authorization to use any material submitted for verification, audit, publication, AI-assisted processing or proof generation.
