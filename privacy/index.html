Privacy Policy

HBCE Platform is designed around data minimization, private identity verification, local-first evidence handling, hash-oriented public verification and fail-closed registry semantics. The current platform is positioned around IPR as the base operational product, IPR AI Audit Trail as the first MVP use case, HBCE as the operational ecosystem, JOKER-C2 as the runtime demonstrator and MATRiX as the architectural framework.

IPR Verified may use tax-code and valid identity-document evidence only in the private verification layer. The public layer is a documentation, proof-reference and verification gateway. It must never become a public custody layer for tax codes, document numbers, identity documents, customer data, private evidence, credentials, secrets, private prompts, private outputs or sensitive operational payloads.

Policy: private verification · GDPR-min · hash-oriented evidence · fail-closed · append-only discipline · no public data custody · EU-first

1. Privacy posture

The privacy model of the public HBCE Platform is based on separating private identity verification from public proof references. Identity evidence may be used privately for IPR Verified, while the public layer must expose only minimized proof material.

  • Private identity verification — tax-code and valid-document evidence may be used only in a private verification workflow.
  • GDPR data minimization — only the minimum public proof material should be exposed.
  • No public identity custody — the public layer must not store full identity records, identity documents, tax codes, document numbers or personal evidence.
  • Hash-oriented public verification — public verification should rely on minimized proof references and digest comparison.
  • Local-first evidence handling — complete evidence files remain under the control of the holder or authorized private process.
  • Append-only registry discipline — public proof records should be extended and reviewed, not silently rewritten.
  • Fail-closed validation — missing, invalid, malformed or ambiguous records must not produce validity.

2. IPR privacy boundary

IPR, or Identity Primary Record, is currently treated as an R&D/MVP operational identity record. IPR Base is a technical R&D record and does not claim verified identity status. IPR Verified is the profile that may use private tax-code and valid identity-document verification.

IPR is not a public identity document, not a civil registry entry, not a regulated KYC / AML service, not a public data vault and not a replacement for SPID, CIE, EUDI Wallet, eIDAS schemes, public registries, digital signatures or qualified trust services.

An IPR-linked workflow may use a public proof reference to support later verification, but the complete identity material, tax code, document number, identity scan, private evidence, legal documents, customer files and sensitive payloads must remain outside the public repository and outside public registry surfaces.

3. Private verification layer

The private verification layer may be used for IPR Verified. It may include tax-code and valid identity-document evidence, but only under a controlled private process with an appropriate legal, privacy and security boundary.

Accepted document categories for the IPR Verified target model are:

  • identity card / CIE;
  • passport;
  • driving license.

The private verification layer may generate a private evidence package and a hash-oriented proof reference, but it must not publish the underlying identity evidence to the public layer.

4. Public registry data

The public registry should contain only minimized public proof records. Under the current registry model, public entries may use fields such as:

  • entity_type
  • subject_label
  • operator_label, where applicable
  • record_scope, where needed
  • verification_level, where needed
  • payload_sha256
  • evt_id, where applicable
  • opc_id, where applicable
  • timestamp
  • status
  • short non-sensitive public notes, where needed

Public labels are not intended to replace private identity verification. A public proof record only means that a minimized public proof reference is present in the public registry.

5. Data not allowed in the public layer

The public repository, public registry, public demo surfaces and public pages must not contain:

  • API keys;
  • private keys;
  • passwords;
  • seed phrases;
  • authentication tokens;
  • credentials;
  • raw personal identifiers;
  • tax codes or fiscal codes;
  • identity document numbers;
  • identity card / CIE numbers;
  • passport numbers;
  • driving license numbers;
  • identity documents;
  • identity document scans or photos;
  • portrait images or biometric material;
  • addresses, birth dates or full civil identity payloads;
  • private identity evidence;
  • private communications;
  • customer data;
  • confidential third-party data;
  • private AI prompts;
  • private AI outputs;
  • client files;
  • production logs containing personal or sensitive data;
  • sensitive operational payloads;
  • private endpoints, private infrastructure details or internal credentials.

6. Local-first evidence handling

Full evidence files, identity materials, tax-code evidence, identity-document evidence, receipt preimages, private proofs, private documents, customer files and sensitive operational records should remain outside the public repository and outside the public registry.

The public layer may publish a hash-oriented commitment or minimized proof reference, but the complete evidence should remain under the control of the holder, maintainer, authorized organization or lawful private review process.

Any external pilot, customer workflow or production environment must define a separate legal basis, data boundary, security boundary, retention model, access-control model and responsibility owner.

7. Demo and pilot data boundary

Public demos and R&D pilot tests should use only synthetic, public or explicitly authorized materials. Do not upload or paste secrets, private keys, credentials, identity documents, tax codes, customer personal data, confidential third-party data, protected business material, private prompts, private outputs or sensitive evidence into public demo surfaces.

If a real deployment requires identity verification, tax-code checks, document checks or sensitive data, the public R&D setup is not sufficient by itself. A dedicated legal, privacy, security and operational environment is required before processing such data.

8. Verification

Verification is performed through deterministic comparison against public proof references, primarily using payload_sha256, registry semantics and local hash comparison.

  • valid public match → PUBLIC_REFERENCE_PRESENT;
  • missing match → NO_PUBLIC_RECORD;
  • invalid input → INVALID;
  • unreachable or malformed registry → NON_OPERATIONAL.

A public match does not replace private evidence review, identity verification, contractual qualification, legal authorization, regulated certification, cybersecurity assurance, institutional validation or production authorization.

9. Cookies, analytics and tracking

The public HBCE Platform should avoid unnecessary tracking and unnecessary analytics. If analytics, cookies or third-party services are introduced in the future, they should be documented clearly and configured consistently with data minimization principles.

Public GitHub Pages hosting may generate standard technical logs under the hosting provider’s own policies. This repository does not use those logs as an identity-custody system and does not treat them as IPR evidence.

10. Security reports and exposed data

If secrets, credentials, personal data, tax codes, identity documents, private evidence, customer material or sensitive operational payloads are discovered in the public repository, public registry or public pages, they should be reported according to SECURITY.md.

Do not copy, redistribute, exploit, index, reuse or amplify exposed sensitive material.
