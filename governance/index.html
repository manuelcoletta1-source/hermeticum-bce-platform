Governance

HBCE Platform defines a governance model for public proof commitments, operational identity research, audit-ready records, responsible AI workflows, and fail-closed verification.

The governance objective is to make system states reviewable, attributable, reconstructible, and resistant to ambiguous validity.

Policy: EU-first · audit-first · registry v3 · payload_sha256 · hash-only · append-only · fail-closed · no public data custody

1. Governance status

HBCE Platform is an experimental R&D public gateway. Its governance model is designed for research, documentation, auditability, public proof verification, and responsible AI governance.

Unless a specific component is separately documented as implemented, validated, and operationally active, the material should be treated as experimental, prototypical, conceptual, or documentary.

Governance language in this repository does not create automatic legal certification, public authority approval, regulated qualification, civil identity status, or institutional authorization.

2. Canonical governance principles

  • EU-first: the European regulatory environment is used as the default governance reference point.
  • Audit-first: relevant records, changes, and decisions should remain reviewable and reconstructible.
  • Fail-closed: missing, malformed, inconsistent, or unverifiable states must not produce implicit validity.
  • Hash-only public proof: the public layer should use minimized proof commitments, primarily payload_sha256.
  • Append-only discipline: public evidence history should be extended through new records or events, not silently rewritten.
  • No public data custody: the public repository and registry must not store private evidence, identity documents, credentials, secrets, private keys, or sensitive operational payloads.
  • Human responsibility: AI-assisted workflows must remain connected to human validation, responsibility ownership, auditability, and policy constraints.

4. Public proof interpretation

What public proof can show

  • a public proof commitment is present;
  • a hash matches an expected commitment;
  • a public timestamped record exists;
  • a public status field has been declared;
  • an audit trace records a review or remediation step.

What public proof does not show by itself

  • it does not certify civil identity;
  • it does not replace private evidence review;
  • it does not create automatic legal authorization;
  • it does not replace institutional validation;
  • it does not replace regulated certification;
  • it does not validate private claims without supporting evidence.

5. AI governance and human responsibility

Within the MATRIX / AI JOKER-C2 research line, AI is treated as a support layer, not as an autonomous replacement for human or institutional responsibility.

Responsible AI workflows should preserve:

  • human validation;
  • responsibility ownership;
  • policy constraints;
  • risk classification;
  • audit metadata;
  • event continuity;
  • public/private data separation;
  • fail-closed escalation when required conditions are missing.

If an AI-assisted workflow cannot preserve accountability, validation, or auditability, it should block, escalate, or require human review.
