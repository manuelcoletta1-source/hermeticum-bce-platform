EU Digital Reality Check

A public, metrological observatory that measures the gap between EU security/accountability requirements and real-world technical verifiability. This program uses only publicly documented information and a reproducible scoring method (HRS-26).

1) Scope (non-political, technical)

EU Digital Reality Check does not attribute responsibility and does not provide legal conclusions. It measures what is technically verifiable from the outside: identity controls, log integrity, deterministic verification, containment, and recovery transparency.

  • Inputs: public disclosures, authority notices, technical reports, specialized media.
  • Outputs: numbered cases, repeatable indicators, patterns, and minimal technical recommendations.
  • Rule: the score reflects external evidence, not private controls.

2) Proof Levels (P0–P3)

P0

Rumor / unverified signals.

P1

Media reporting with partial confirmation.

P2

Public confirmation (organization or authority), minimal technical detail.

P3

Official disclosure with timeline, scope, mitigations, and authority-facing reporting.

3) HRS-26 (formal definition)

HRS-26 is a 0–100 score computed as a sum of five indices (0–20 each): HRS-26 = I1 + I2 + I3 + I4 + I5.

I1 — Identity

Operational identity strength and role separation.

I2 — Integrity

Logging integrity, immutability, measurability of scope.

I3 — Verification

Deterministic verification: hashes, signatures, independently replicable checks.

I4 — Containment

Blast-radius limitation, segmentation, third-party exposure control.

I5 — Recovery

Disclosure, transparency, mitigation quality, and measurable hardening.

Disclaimer: HRS-26 measures only publicly evidenced verifiability (external observability score). It does not measure moral responsibility, private controls, or legal liability.

4) Pattern Library

  • P-A Central DB Magnet (high-value centralized datasets)
  • P-B Identity/Access Weak Link (phishing, SSO, social engineering)
  • P-C Mutable Logs (audit fragile, retroactive uncertainty)
  • P-D Third-Party Spillover (processors/suppliers contamination)
  • P-E Low-Friction Fraud (business logic / workflow manipulation)
  • P-F Unknown Scope (uncertain perimeters at disclosure time)
  • P-G AI Misuse (AI used as attack infrastructure or obfuscation)
  • P-H Infrastructure Misconfiguration (cloud/IAM/storage errors)

6) Official Position

HERMETICUM B.C.E. S.r.l. recognizes the importance of EU regulatory frameworks (GDPR, NIS2, eIDAS, AI governance). This observatory evaluates technical implementability and public verifiability. It does not replace legal frameworks and does not provide enforcement services.

The central claim is technical: many systems cannot yet provide deterministic, independently verifiable evidence of identity, state, and event integrity.
