Discussion of private verification boundaries, identity-evidence separation, public proof minimization and GDPR-oriented data minimization.

A controlled 30-day pilot can test one document workflow with 10–20 public, synthetic, owned or expressly authorized documents.

5. Minimum conditions for discussion

Real capability

  • availability for technical review or controlled pilot scoping;
  • ability to define non-sensitive test material;
  • capacity to review technical, legal, security or governance boundaries;
  • commitment to clear timeline and deliverables.

Principle alignment

  • verification through explicit fail-closed states;
  • public proof minimization;
  • no public custody of identity evidence;
  • human responsibility and auditability;
  • no overclaiming of adoption, certification, customer status or authority.

Expected outputs

  • pilot use case definition;
  • data boundary definition;
  • integration questions;
  • test reports and validation metrics;
  • production-readiness gaps.

6. Contact boundary

In a first communication, include only non-sensitive information: organization type, country, domain, possible pilot capability and technical contact role.

Do not send tax codes, document numbers, identity documents, identity scans, customer files, secrets, credentials, private keys, production logs, private evidence or confidential third-party material through preliminary contact channels.
