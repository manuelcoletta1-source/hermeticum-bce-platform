Claims & Non-Claims

HBCE Platform is an experimental R&D public gateway for operational identity research, minimized public proof, registry v3 verification, audit-ready records, and responsible AI governance.

This page defines what HBCE claims, what it does not claim, and where the boundaries of the public platform are. Clear boundaries reduce ambiguity, prevent overclaiming, and make audit review easier.

Policy: R&D · EU-first · audit-first · registry v3 · payload_sha256 · hash-only · fail-closed · no public data custody

Baseline Standards Tech Position Legal Audit Findings

1. Status of the claims

The claims on this page describe the intended technical and governance posture of the public HBCE Platform. They should be understood as R&D, documentation, prototype, and public-governance positioning unless a specific component is separately documented as implemented, validated, and operationally active.

These claims do not create automatic legal certification, regulated certification, public authority approval, civil identity status, qualified trust service status, or institutional authorization.

2. What HBCE provides

Minimized public proof

HBCE provides a public proof model based on minimized registry records and hash commitments. The central public proof field is payload_sha256.

Registry v3 semantics

HBCE defines registry v3 semantics for public entries using minimized fields such as entity_type, subject_label, payload_sha256, timestamp, and status.

Deterministic verification

HBCE verification is designed around deterministic checks: public proof presence, hash comparison, schema validation, and explicit PASS / FAIL style outcomes.

Fail-closed governance

HBCE treats missing, malformed, inconsistent, unverifiable, or ambiguous states as invalid, non-operational, or requiring manual/private review.

Audit-ready documentation

HBCE uses public audit traces, issue records, findings registers, schemas, commits, and policy files to make repository hardening and governance posture reviewable.

Responsible AI governance model

HBCE supports the MATRIX / AI JOKER-C2 research line by framing AI as a decision-support layer that must remain linked to human validation, responsibility ownership, policy constraints, and auditability.

3. What HBCE does not claim

Not a legal identity scheme

HBCE does not claim to replace eIDAS, EUDI Wallet, SPID, CIE, national eID systems, civil registries, or qualified trust services. HBCE is an experimental operational identity and public proof framework.

Not KYC and not a public data vault

HBCE does not provide public custody of personal data. The public layer is designed for minimized hash-only proof commitments, not for storing full identity evidence.

Not automatic legal certification

A public proof match does not automatically create legal certification, institutional authorization, regulated qualification, tax recognition, contractual status, or public authority approval.

Not a replacement for cybersecurity

HBCE does not replace secure development, SOC, incident response, endpoint protection, access control, network security, vulnerability management, or security operations. It adds auditability and proof continuity to specific workflows.

Not a “trust me” system

HBCE does not rely on brand trust or narrative trust as a substitute for verification. Public proof should be checked through deterministic registry, schema, and hash logic.

Not reputation scoring

HBCE does not claim to rank individuals, operators, organizations, or systems by reputation. It provides public proof commitments and continuity references that can support later audit.

Not surveillance infrastructure

HBCE does not require continuous tracking of individuals. The public layer should contain minimized public proof records, not personal telemetry or behavioral tracking.

Not autonomous authority

HBCE does not authorize AI systems to replace human or institutional responsibility. AI-assisted workflows must remain subject to human validation, auditability, and responsibility ownership.

4. Public proof interpretation

What public proof can show

  • a public proof commitment is present;
  • a hash matches an expected commitment;
  • a timestamped public record exists;
  • a public status field is declared;
  • a public audit trace records a review, finding, or remediation step.

What public proof does not show by itself

  • it does not certify civil identity;
  • it does not replace private evidence review;
  • it does not create automatic legal authorization;
  • it does not replace institutional validation;
  • it does not replace regulated certification;
  • it does not validate private claims without supporting evidence.

5. Public data boundaries

The public layer must not contain identity documents, raw personal identifiers, private evidence, credentials, secrets, private keys, production logs, private communications, or sensitive operational payloads.

Public records should be limited to minimized fields such as:

  • entity_type
  • subject_label
  • operator_label, where applicable
  • record_scope
  • payload_sha256
  • timestamp
  • status

Complete evidence, identity material, legal documents, credentials, and sensitive payloads must remain outside the public repository and registry.

6. Why boundaries matter

In high-accountability environments, ambiguity becomes risk. Boundary clarity makes adoption, audit, review, and technical integration safer because the scope of each claim is explicit.

HBCE Platform uses these boundaries to prevent overclaiming, reduce privacy risk, and separate public proof from legal, institutional, contractual, or private validation processes.
