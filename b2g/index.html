The current concrete MVP is MATRIX AI Audit Trail. It demonstrates a narrow and testable public-sector-relevant workflow: AI-assisted document analysis with document hash, governance decision, human validation, EVT event, OPC proof and audit-ready report.

3. Public-sector problem addressed

Public-sector and regulated organizations may increasingly use AI to support document analysis, policy review, cybersecurity documentation, compliance workflows, procurement support, civil-protection documentation and administrative decision support.

Ordinary AI use may not be sufficiently traceable. A team may not be able to prove which document was analyzed, what AI action was requested, what risk class was assigned, whether a human reviewed the output, and whether a final audit trail exists.

MATRIX AI Audit Trail addresses this gap by making the AI-assisted workflow reviewable and reportable.

4. Public-sector relevant principles

HUMAN RESPONSIBILITY

AI is decision-support

AI output must not replace human or institutional responsibility. Human validation remains explicit.

AUDITABILITY

Traceable workflow

Document hash, AI action, governance decision, EVT, OPC and report make the workflow reviewable.

FAIL-CLOSED

No implicit validity

Missing, malformed or unverifiable records must not produce validity or authorization.

NO-CUSTODY

Public proof only

The public layer should not store private evidence, identity documents, credentials or sensitive data.

REGISTRY v3

payload_sha256

Public proof records should use minimized registry v3 semantics and payload_sha256.

EU-FIRST

Governance reference

EU-first is a design and governance reference posture, not a formal certification or public authority approval.

6. Pilot path

A responsible public-sector-relevant pilot should start small:

  • duration: 30 days;
  • users: 1–3 authorized operators;
  • documents: 10–20 public, synthetic or explicitly authorized documents;
  • workflow: one AI-assisted document analysis process;
  • output: audit-ready report package;
  • review: usability, traceability, risk classification, validation step and report clarity.

Any pilot involving real public-sector data, regulated data, personal data or sensitive operational data would require prior legal, security, privacy and institutional review.
