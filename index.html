HBCE is a public gateway for verifiable infrastructure research

HBCE Platform is an experimental R&D public gateway designed to document and test a verifiable infrastructure model: operational identity, minimized public proof, deterministic verification, append-only records, audit-ready evidence, and governed AI workflow documentation.

The public layer is based on payload_sha256, registry v3 semantics, hash-only proof commitments, no-public-data-custody rules, and fail-closed verification.

A public proof match means that a public proof commitment is present. It does not replace private evidence review, legal authorization, institutional validation, regulated certification, contractual qualification, or civil identity verification.

PUBLIC GATEWAY LIMIT

This gateway is a public documentation, registry, verification, and audit surface. It is not a secure backend, not a public identity-custody layer, not a legal certification layer, and not an unrestricted autonomous AI system.

Public verification flow

STEP 01

Create or hold evidence

Evidence, receipts, manifests, or operational records should remain private or controlled unless explicitly safe for public release. Public records should expose only minimized proof commitments.

Open Create
STEP 02

Publish minimized proof

The public registry should contain minimized records based on registry v3 fields such as entity_type, subject_label, payload_sha256, timestamp, and status.

Open Registry
STEP 03

Verify deterministically

Verification returns explicit states such as PUBLIC_RECORD_PRESENT, NO_PUBLIC_RECORD, INVALID, or NON_OPERATIONAL. No match means no public proof record.

Minimal public proof chain

HBCE treats public proof as a reconstructible commitment chain. Public records do not need to expose private evidence. The minimal public chain is: 

PRIVATE OR CONTROLLED EVIDENCE
↓
CANONICAL HASH COMMITMENT
↓
PAYLOAD_SHA256
↓
PUBLIC REGISTRY v3 RECORD
↓
DETERMINISTIC VERIFY
↓
PUBLIC_RECORD_PRESENT / NO_PUBLIC_RECORD / INVALID / NON_OPERATIONAL

This chain supports public proof presence and auditability. It does not automatically certify identity, legal rights, institutional authorization, or regulated status.

Why this gateway exists

Modern systems can execute, but often fail to preserve reliable historical structure. Identities fragment, logs mutate, and decision chains become difficult to reconstruct.

HBCE exists to explore a different model: a public research environment where proof commitments can be checked, registry records can be reviewed, and AI-assisted governance can remain connected to human responsibility, auditability, and fail-closed validation.
