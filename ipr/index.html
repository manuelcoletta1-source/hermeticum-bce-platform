What IPR is

Operational identity, attribution and proof

IPR is not a simple digital identity, login, account, credential or social profile. It is an operational reference designed to connect a subject to a verifiable sequence of actions.

Subject — the human, organization, AI system, agent, node or process associated with the record.
Role — the operational capacity in which the subject acts.
Operation — the action, document workflow, AI interaction or process being recorded.
Event — the EVT layer that records the relevant state transition.
Proof — the hash, OPC reference or verification surface connected to the operation.
Continuity — the trace that links one operation to the next.
Responsibility — the attribution boundary that makes the operation reviewable.

What IPR is not

It is not a public KYC database.
It is not a public data vault.
It is not a regulated identity provider by itself.
It is not an eIDAS qualified trust service.
It is not legal certification by itself.
It is not automated compliance.
It is not a substitute for private legal, cybersecurity, privacy or compliance review.
Public boundary: R&D reference surface · hash-only public proof · no public data custody · no private key custody · no regulated certification claim.
FUNCTIONAL ANALOGY

IBAN, tax code and IPR

An IBAN identifies a bank account inside the financial system. A tax code identifies a subject inside the administrative and fiscal system. A digital signature validates an electronic act. A credential allows access to a service.

IPR is different from all of these, but it plays a comparable ordering role inside a governed digital ecosystem. It identifies an operational subject inside HBCE and connects that subject not only to access, but to operations, events, proofs, responsibility and continuity.

IPR does not claim the current legal status of a tax code, the banking function of an IBAN or the regulated effect of a qualified digital signature. The analogy is functional: complex systems require stable reference instruments to organize identity, operations and responsibility. Inside HBCE, that operational reference instrument is IPR.
