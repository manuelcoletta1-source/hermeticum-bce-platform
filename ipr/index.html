IPR is a verified operational identity record.

IPR — Identity Primary Record — is the first operational product of the HBCE research infrastructure. It is designed as an operational identity, attribution and proof record created through a private identity-verification workflow and exposed publicly only through minimized proof references.

For a human IPR, the target verification model uses a tax code and one valid identity document: identity card / CIE, passport or driving license. The public registry does not store identity documents, tax codes, document numbers or personal payloads. It stores only hash-oriented proof references.

What IPR is

Verified operational identity, attribution and proof

IPR is not a simple digital identity, login, account, credential or social profile. It is an operational reference designed to connect a subject to a verifiable sequence of actions. In the target verified model, a human IPR begins from private identity verification based on a tax code and a valid identity document.

Subject — the human, organization, AI system, agent, node or process associated with the record.
Private verification — the private check that may use tax-code and valid identity-document evidence.
Role — the operational capacity in which the subject acts.
Operation — the action, document workflow, AI interaction or process being recorded.
Event — the EVT layer that records the relevant state transition.
Proof — the hash, OPC reference or verification surface connected to the operation.
Continuity — the trace that links one operation to the next.
Responsibility — the attribution boundary that makes the operation reviewable.

PRIVATE VERIFICATION MODEL

Tax code + valid identity document

The target creation model for a serious human IPR follows a bank-app style verification pattern: the subject provides a tax code and one valid identity document. Accepted document categories are identity card / CIE, passport or driving license.

This verification material belongs to the private evidence layer. It must not be published in the public registry, public repository, public demo pages or public proof records.

FUNCTIONAL ANALOGY

IBAN, tax code and IPR

An IBAN identifies a bank account inside the financial system. A tax code identifies a subject inside the administrative and fiscal system. A digital signature validates an electronic act. A credential allows access to a service.

IPR is different from all of these, but it plays a comparable ordering role inside a governed digital ecosystem. It identifies an operational subject inside HBCE and connects that subject not only to access, but to operations, events, proofs, responsibility and continuity.

IPR does not claim the current legal status of a tax code, the banking function of an IBAN, the official status of a public identity document or the regulated effect of a qualified digital signature. The analogy is functional: complex systems require stable reference instruments to organize identity, operations and responsibility. Inside HBCE, that operational reference instrument is IPR.

IPR types
