Legal & policy overview

This page summarizes the legal and policy posture of HBCE Platform as an experimental R&D public gateway for hash-only public proof, audit-ready documentation, registry v3 verification, and fail-closed governance.

This page is informational only. It is not legal advice, regulatory certification, fiscal advice, contractual advice, or automatic institutional authorization.

1. Project status

HBCE Platform is currently an experimental research and development public infrastructure. It documents and tests models for operational identity, public proof commitments, registry verification, audit-ready records, and fail-closed governance.

Unless a specific component is separately documented as implemented, validated, and operationally active, the materials should be understood as R&D, prototype, conceptual, or documentary material.

2. Core principles

EU-first

The European regulatory environment is treated as the default policy reference point for governance analysis. This does not mean that the platform is certified, approved, or endorsed by any public authority.

Audit-first

Relevant public records should remain reviewable, reconstructible, and documented through audit traces, issue records, public proof commitments, and append-only change history.

Fail-closed

Missing, malformed, inconsistent, unverifiable, or ambiguous states must not produce implicit validity. When a required condition is not satisfied, the workflow should return INVALID, NO_PUBLIC_RECORD, NON_OPERATIONAL, or require private/manual review.

Hash-only & append-only

The public registry should publish minimized proof commitments, primarily through payload_sha256, timestamps, public labels, and public status metadata. Public proof history should be extended rather than silently rewritten.

3. Public proof does not replace private validation

A match in the public registry means that a public proof commitment is present. It does not replace:

  • private evidence review;
  • identity verification;
  • contractual qualification;
  • legal authorization;
  • institutional validation;
  • regulated certification;
  • tax or accounting documentation;
  • public authority approval.

Public verification should be read as proof commitment verification, not as automatic legal certification.

4. GDPR minimization and data handling

The platform is designed to minimize what is exposed publicly and to keep private evidence outside public channels. The public layer should demonstrate integrity and public proof presence without revealing private evidence or sensitive personal data.

Public layer

  • public proof commitments only;
  • payload_sha256 as the primary public proof field;
  • minimized labels such as subject_label and operator_label;
  • timestamps and public lifecycle status;
  • no private evidence custody;
  • no identity documents;
  • no credentials, secrets, tokens, passwords, or private keys.

Private or operational layer

  • complete evidence should remain under holder or authorized-process control;
  • private verification should use controlled and lawful channels;
  • only the minimum necessary information should be shared;
  • sensitive material must not be placed in the public repository or public registry.

Practical rule: never send or publish secrets, passwords, seed phrases, private keys, raw identity documents, authentication tokens, private evidence, or sensitive operational payloads in public issues, public registry records, public pages, or public commits.

5. No public identity custody

HBCE Platform does not position the public registry as a public identity-custody database. The registry is a public proof layer for minimized proof commitments.

Practical interpretation

  • the public registry is not a civil registry;
  • the public registry does not replace official documents;
  • the public registry does not publish private identity evidence;
  • the public registry supports integrity and public-proof lookup through hash matching;
  • complete evidence remains outside the public layer;
  • private validation requires separate controlled processes.

6. Defensive use and prohibited use

HBCE Platform should be used only for lawful, authorized, defensive, audit-oriented, research, documentation, verification, and governance purposes.

The platform must not be used for unauthorized access, offensive cybersecurity activity, exploitation, malware development, credential theft, evasion, data exfiltration, destructive testing, unlawful surveillance, or harmful automation.

7. Liability and no warranty

The platform is provided as experimental R&D material and public documentation. It is not provided as production-grade infrastructure, legal advice, regulatory certification, fiscal advice, cybersecurity certification, professional assurance, or institutional authorization.

Users are responsible for verifying whether any use of the platform is appropriate, lawful, authorized, and compatible with their own operational, legal, contractual, fiscal, and regulatory requirements.
