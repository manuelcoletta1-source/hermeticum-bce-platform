1. Core thesis

If a system cannot reconstruct who or what acted, under which context, with which proof and with which responsibility, it cannot be considered operationally reliable in high-accountability environments.

HBCE defines an R&D technical posture for IPR-linked workflows: operational identity, private verification boundaries, public proof minimization, append-only event continuity and fail-closed verification.

This is a technical design posture. It is not a legal opinion, regulatory certification, public authority standard, qualified trust-service designation or production compliance claim.

4. IPR Verified boundary

IPR Verified may use private identity verification based on tax-code and valid identity-document evidence. Accepted document categories are identity card / CIE, passport and driving license.

This evidence belongs only in the private verification layer. The public layer must not store tax codes, document numbers, identity documents, scans, portrait images, biometric material, private evidence, personal data or customer payloads. 

PRIVATE VERIFICATION
→ TAX CODE + VALID IDENTITY DOCUMENT
→ PRIVATE EVIDENCE PACKAGE
→ IPR VERIFIED RECORD
→ HASH-ORIENTED PUBLIC PROOF REFERENCE
→ VERIFY / AUDIT / EVT / OPC

IPR Verified strengthens operational seriousness, but it does not create legal identity, regulated KYC / AML compliance, eIDAS qualification, public authority approval or production authorization by itself.

5. Non-negotiable invariants

  • PRIVATE VERIFICATION SEPARATION — identity evidence belongs only in a private controlled layer.
  • PUBLIC PROOF MINIMIZATION — public records expose proof references, not private payloads.
  • HASH-ORIENTED PUBLIC LAYER — public verification uses digest commitments such as payload_sha256.
  • APPEND-ONLY TRAJECTORY — public history should be extended, not silently rewritten.
  • DETERMINISTIC VERIFICATION — verification should return explicit states.
  • FAIL-CLOSED ENFORCEMENT — missing, malformed or forbidden data must not produce validity.
  • NO PUBLIC DATA CUSTODY — no tax codes, document numbers, identity documents or private evidence in public files.
  • HUMAN RESPONSIBILITY — AI outputs remain decision-support unless separately validated by accountable humans.

6. Why this emerges now

High-accountability environments require traceability, responsibility and reproducibility.

AI systems

Model outputs without identity continuity and human validation create accountability gaps.

Document workflows

AI-assisted review without proof references creates weak auditability.

Critical documentation

Logs without append-only and fail-closed verification cannot be treated as definitive.

Public-sector contexts

Institutional AI workflows require explicit human responsibility and private/public evidence separation.

7. EU compatibility posture

HBCE is designed for structural compatibility with EU-oriented governance principles: minimization, auditability, traceability, human oversight, operational resilience and controlled AI use.

This is an architecture posture, not a legal interpretation or formal certification.

GDPR

No public tax-code custody, no public identity-document custody and minimized public proof references.

NIS2

Deterministic evidence may support incident accountability, but does not create compliance by itself.

eIDAS

Adjacency only. IPR does not replace eIDAS, EUDI Wallet, qualified signatures or qualified trust services.

AI Act

Supports traceability and human validation objectives, but does not automate regulatory compliance.

8. Adoption logic

When operational identity reduces ambiguity and audit cost, it becomes valuable for organizations that must explain how AI-supported work was performed.

  • Lower audit ambiguity.
  • More deterministic document workflows.
  • Clearer human validation boundaries.
  • Public proof without public identity custody.

Adoption still requires business validation, legal review, security review, privacy review and operational readiness.
